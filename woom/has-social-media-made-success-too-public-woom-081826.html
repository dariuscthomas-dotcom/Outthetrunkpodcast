Has Social Media Made Success Too Public? (WOOM 08/18/26)

Show Notes & Highlights

Topics Covered

  • A Different World reboot and why modern reboots feel too serious
  • Netflix content, streaming fatigue, and “homework television”
  • Adult tasks that feel performative: ironing, making beds, folding clothes, sorting laundry, and vacuuming
  • Weight changes, clothing sizes, and accepting when clothes no longer fit
  • Organization, personal systems, and building useful infrastructure
  • Whether social media has made success too public
  • Oversharing wins, losses, remote-work habits, and personal drama online
  • Forced contrarianism and why people resist popular entertainment
  • Mainstream versus niche tastes in music, movies, sports, and food
  • “Washed” culture and exaggerated narratives about aging
  • Popular artists they do not understand and the transition to legacy-act status
  • Creative decline and knowing when a franchise should end

Best Questions

  • Have we made success too public?
  • Which everyday adult responsibilities are unnecessary or performative?
  • Why do people feel pressured to dislike popular things?
  • Who is universally praised in entertainment that you simply do not understand?
  • When should artists and franchises stop producing new work and protect their legacy?

Best Observations

  • Many adult tasks continue because they are traditional, not because they create meaningful value.
  • Social media encourages excessive victory laps and excessive public venting.
  • Popular things usually become popular because they genuinely resonate with many people.
  • Being different is not inherently smarter than enjoying mainstream culture.
  • People often exaggerate aging while ignoring habits that could improve their health and quality of life.

Best Debate

Has social media made success too public? Jordan argued that sharing wins can inspire people and demonstrate what is possible. Darius argued that excessive visibility creates comparison, invites unwanted attention, and encourages oversharing.

Major Themes

  • Adulthood
  • Responsibility vs. convenience
  • Social media etiquette
  • Privacy
  • Remote-work culture
  • Mainstream vs. niche interests
  • Entertainment criticism
  • Aging and self-perception
